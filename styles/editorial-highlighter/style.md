# EDITORIAL HIGHLIGHTER

Feels like an annotated magazine page over a calm sit-down video. The speaker stays put; the page around them keeps changing.

Sizes are for a 1080x1920 frame.

## COLOURS

| Name        | Hex       | Use                                       |
| ----------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Highlighter | `#E8F03A` | key words, big serif words, titles        |
| White       | `#FFFFFF` | captions                                  |
| Ink         | `#111111` | text on white labels                      |
| Alarm red   | `#C8102E` | tag pills, cross marks, handwritten notes |

No other colours in text or graphics. Pictures keep their own colours.

## FONTS

- **Sans:** Inter Tight, Semibold, letter spacing −2%. Captions, labels, pills.
- **Serif:** Instrument Serif, regular and italic. Big words and titles.
- **Hand:** any marker-style handwriting font, all caps. Side notes only.

All free on Google Fonts.

## TEXT TYPES

### CAPTION (MOST OF THE VIDEO)

- Sans, white, about 44px, no box, no outline, a soft shadow at most.
- Centred, **top**, above the speaker's head. If the speaker fills the frame, **off-center**, just below the chin, instead.
- 1–5 words, one line, two at most. The whole phrase appears at once, in time with the voice.
- No punctuation (apostrophes and hyphens inside words stay). No capital at the start of a sentence; names and "I" keep theirs.
- One key word per phrase in Highlighter: the word that carries the meaning (a concept, a number, a name), not the word being spoken. Skip it when no word stands out; about half the captions have one.
- Very rarely, the key word is also italic for a softer emphasis.

### BIG WORD

- Serif, Highlighter, 110–150px, often italic.
- One to three words for an emotional or important beat. It replaces the caption on the same frame: the caption is gone the moment the first big word appears, and comes back only after the big word is gone. Never both at once.
- Shows one word at a time as it's spoken. Each word lands somewhere new: left, centre, right, staggered like a poem.
- About one every 5–10 seconds. Never two in a row.

### LABEL

- Sans, Ink, about 36px, on a white box, 6px corners, tight padding.
- Used instead of the caption when the top of the frame is busy (picture, chart, full-screen footage) or the background is too bright for white text.
- Centred, **off-center** (chest height), or just below whatever made the frame busy.

### TAG PILLS

- Sans, white, about 34px, on Alarm red, fully round ends, thin light outline.
- For lists of things that are named: three to five words, popping in one by one, scattered loosely around the head.

### CROSS LIST

- Small white sans words, each with a red cross above.
- For "no this, no that" lists. Items appear one by one, scattered.

### SIDE NOTE

- Hand font, Alarm red, small, tilted a few degrees.
- A joke or aside next to a picture. Rare: once per video at most.

### STICKER WORD

- Serif, Ink, on a white rectangle, no rounded corners.
- One word stuck on top of a screenshot or someone else's clip.

## TITLE (FIRST 3–5 SECONDS)

- Two lines at the top: a short sans line in white (about 44px), then the hook in big Highlighter serif (about 120px).
- Optional: a short kicker in white caps sans on a black rounded box underneath.
- Captions keep running below the title while it's up. The title cuts away when the first section starts.
- Optional section headers later: serif, white, on an Alarm red rectangle, with a number ("2. …").

## PICTURES

Pictures are the main event: something new on almost every line.

- **Cards:** rectangle, no border, no shadow, no rounded corners, 40–60% of the frame width. Placed over the speaker's chest.
- **Cutouts:** people or objects with the background removed, standing in the bottom corners in front of the speaker.
- **Collages:** two to four cards scattered around the speaker at once.
- **Screenshots:** articles, posts, quotes, apps, shown as cards, readable.
- **Small icons and doodles:** 3D icons, white line doodles, tiny animated bits next to the head. Playful, small.
- **Full-screen footage (cutaway):** the speaker's own life or other clips for a few seconds. Captions become labels.

Matching rule: the picture shows the literal thing being said (a name shows their photo, a number shows a chart, a feeling shows a film scene).

## PACING AND CUTS

- Pauses removed with jump cuts. At cuts, switch between the normal frame and a punch-in (about 115–125%, face kept in the same place). Hide the cut behind a new caption or picture where possible.
- Something changes on screen every 1–2 seconds: caption, picture or big word.
- Full-screen footage: a few times per video, 2–5 seconds each.
- Busy but not crowded: one line of spoken text at a time (a caption, a label or a big word, never two), plus at most one picture group.

## MOTION

Everything comes in quickly and visibly, then leaves on a hard cut.

- **Cards:** slide in from the side or from below with motion blur, 5–6 frames, fast start and soft landing.
- **Small cards and cutouts:** grow from about 30% while sliding into place, 5–6 frames, then float very gently.
- **Big words:** fade in with a little blur, 2–3 frames, one word at a time.
- **Captions and labels:** cut in, no animation.
- **Pills, crosses, labels added one by one, icons:** pop in from about 30% to 100% over 4–6 frames, a tiny overshoot.
- **Small doodles and icons:** may wiggle or loop gently.
- **Leaving:** hard cut, usually together with the caption change.

## SOUND

- No music. The voice carries the video.
- Very subtle sound effects on pictures, pops and big words: soft clicks, pops and swishes, felt more than heard.

## ENDING

- No end card. The last line is a question or call to action, as a caption with the key word in Highlighter.

## DON'T

- No bottom captions, no word-by-word karaoke, no boxes behind normal captions.
- No colours outside the palette.
- No shakes, glitches, spins or flashy transitions between clips.
