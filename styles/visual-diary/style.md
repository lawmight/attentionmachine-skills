# Visual Diary

Feels like a hand-laid page from someone's diary. Warm footage, white type placed with care, and the camera often leaves the speaker to show what they're talking about.

Sizes are for a 1080x1920 frame.

## Colours

- White `#FFFFFF` for all text. No shadows, no boxes, no outlines; pick shots where white reads.
- No other colours in text or graphics. Footage keeps its own colours, ideally warm.

## Fonts

- **Sans:** light, round and geometric (Lexend Light, Quicksand). Small lines.
- **Serif:** chunky, soft display serif (DM Serif Display, Young Serif). Big words.
- **Script:** thin calligraphy (Pinyon Script, Italianno). One accent word at a time.

All free on Google Fonts.

## Text types

### Caption (most of the video)

- Sans, white, small: about 32px.
- Centred, **top**, a little lower: just above the speaker's head.
- Builds word by word as it's spoken, one line of up to about six words, then clears for the next phrase.
- All lowercase, even "i". Keeps normal punctuation (commas, colons, apostrophes) and the odd typed emoticon ("<3").

### Type composition (the big moments)

- Two to four lines in different fonts and sizes, set as one shape: a small sans line (about 40px), a big serif line (about 110–150px), sometimes a script word.
- Lines fit into each other. A short small line tucks into the empty space above or beside the start of a big line, sharing its edge. No even line spacing; it should look placed by hand.
- Built word by word as spoken. It replaces the caption while it's up.
- Usually **top**, over the head. For a list of words, scatter them around the head and face, never across eyes or mouth.
- For hooks, greetings, numbers and the one phrase that matters in each part.

### Script accent

- One word in script, often the first word of a composition (a greeting, "every", "&"), or the number of a step ("1.").

### Step label

- A tiny script number or sparkle, then a serif word or two: "1. write a script". **top**. For a process told in steps.

## Title (first seconds)

- A type composition with the hook, in the first second, over the head.

## Pictures

- Mostly footage, not stills: the real thing being talked about (the desk, the app, the finished result).
- Screenshots of documents or apps appear inside a split, readable.

## Layouts

- **cutaway:** footage of the process or the place, a few seconds, the voice keeps going. Captions keep running.
- **split:** 50/50. The thing being explained on top (a document, a screen recording, an app), the speaker cropped into the bottom half. Captions sit on the seam.
- **inset:** a small portrait clip, about 30% of the width, rounded corners (about 24px), floating over the speaker's chest, centred. Shows the result being talked about.
- Something other than the plain talking shot every 5–10 seconds.

## Pacing and cuts

- Hard cuts. Pauses removed.
- Punch-ins (about 115–130%) now and then, to grab attention on a point, not on every cut and not to hide cuts.
- Calm overall: text changes with the words, the picture every few seconds.

## Motion

- Text cuts in, word by word. No slides, no pops, no bouncing.
- Insets and splits cut in and out.
- The only motion is in the footage and the punch-ins.

## Sound

- A soft bell or ding on the hook and on the line that matters most. Once or twice per video.
- No music under the speaking. Light music comes in only at the very end.

## Ending

- A warm gesture to camera (a wave, a heart), with a small lowercase call to follow, in sans with a script accent.

## Don't

- No coloured or highlighted words, no boxes, no shadows.
- No capitals, except what the style shows in compositions.
- No fast zooms, shakes, spins or transitions.
- No karaoke highlighting.
