# Show and Tell

Feels like a thoughtful friend telling a story and holding up photos as they go. Plain, warm and grown-up: no colour, no gimmicks, just pictures and words.

Sizes are for a 1080x1920 frame.

## Colours

- White `#FFFFFF` for all text, with a faint soft shadow.
- No other colours in text or graphics. Pictures keep their own colours.

## Fonts

- **Caption serif:** a narrow, elegant serif (Instrument Serif). Captions.
- **Display serif:** a sturdy text serif (Newsreader, Fraunces), regular and semibold. Hook, stacks and numbers.

All free on Google Fonts.

## Text types

### Caption (the whole video)

- Caption serif, white, about 48px, one line.
- Short phrases of two to five words, the whole phrase at once, in time with the voice.
- Normal sentence case and punctuation, as written.
- Centred, **bottom**: low on the chest. Stays put the whole video, also under pictures.

### Contrast stack

- Two or three stacked words for the core idea, built word by word as spoken: big word (about 120px), small word (about 60px), big word. Display serif, regular, lowercase.
- Two stacks back to back make the contrast ("more / time / building", then "less / time / buying" in shape).
- Centred, **top**.

### Big number

- A money figure or count, display serif, about 130px, rounded and easy to read ("~1000"). Converted to the viewer's currency if it helps.
- Centred, **top**, for a second or two while it's said.

## Title (first 3–5 seconds)

- Two lines, centred, **top**: the problem as a short lowercase question in display serif (about 60px), then the answer in display serif semibold caps (about 90px).
- Stays while the first sentences play, then cuts away.

## Pictures

The main event: whenever a company, place, thing or scene is named, show a photo of it.

- Laid right over the speaker's face, covering it. About 60–70% of the frame width, landscape, hard edges, no border, no shadow.
- Placed around the head, not always dead centre: shifted left, centre or right from one photo to the next.
- Several in a row for a list ("a night out, a dinner, new shoes"), one per item, changing on the spoken word.
- The caption stays visible below.
- Real photos: logos on buildings, streets, venues, products. Not icons or illustrations.

## Layouts

- Speaker full screen, medium shot, chest up. No splits, insets or cutaways.

## Pacing and cuts

- Tight jump cuts, almost no pauses left.
- Cuts swap between the normal frame and a punch-in (about 115–120%).
- Pictures always arrive on a punch-in.
- Something changes every one to two seconds: caption, picture or punch-in.

## Motion

- Everything cuts in and out: captions, pictures, stacks, numbers. No fades, slides or pops.
- Stack words appear one by one, cut in.

## Sound

- Voice only. No music, no sound effects. The tight cuts carry the rhythm.

## Ending

- The last sentence is the takeaway. No end card, no call to action on screen.

## Don't

- No coloured or highlighted words, no boxes, no outlines.
- No animated text or transitions.
- No cartoon icons or emojis.
