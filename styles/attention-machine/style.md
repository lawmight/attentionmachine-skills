# ATTENTION MACHINE

Feels like a confident friend holding up proof as they talk, in the brand's colours. Calm cutting, photos over the face, heavy type and a clear brand palette.

Sizes are for a 1080x1920 frame.

## COLOURS

| Name | Hex | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | `#0731F8` | The main colour: the block behind every headline (title name, numbers, stack words) |
| Secondary | `#FED347` | The payoff, as text on a blue block: the last big word of a stack, the money figure. Nothing else |
| Cream | `#F5F1EA` | Split panel background behind dark things |
| Dark | `#050505` | Split panel background behind light things |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Captions and small text over footage, with a faint soft shadow; headline text on blue blocks |

- A split panel is cream or dark, whichever stands apart from what's on it: light screenshots and white pages on dark, dark footage and dark interfaces on cream.
- Pictures and clips keep their own colours.
- **Blue block:** every Unbounded headline sits on a solid primary block hugging the text (about 0.1em top and bottom, 0.35em each side, corners about 0.18em). No shadow on the text. One block per line, about 10px apart.
- The **title** (the hook) starts about 80px under the safe zone's top edge, never right on it. It may overlap the top of the head, never the eyes. Everything else on **top** sits where the named position says.

## FONTS

- **Headline:** Unbounded Bold. Title, stacks, numbers.
- **Text:** Poppins Bold. Captions and the title's question line.

Both are free on Google Fonts.

Unbounded is very wide. Size it to fit the safe zone, not to a fixed number.

## TEXT TYPES

### CAPTION (THE WHOLE VIDEO)

- Poppins Bold, white, about 46px, one line, at most about 22 characters.
- On a small dark pill hugging the text: dark at 15% opacity, about 0.4em padding each side, corners about 0.25em.
- Short phrases of two to five words, the whole phrase at once, in time with the voice.
- All lowercase, even at the start of a sentence; only names, brands and "I" keep a capital. Never any punctuation (no full stops, commas or question marks). Apostrophes stay: "don't", "it's".
- Centred, **bottom**. Stays put the whole video, also under pictures.
- During a split screen, captions sit centred on the seam between the two halves.

### CONTRAST STACK

- Two or three stacked words for the core idea, built word by word as spoken: big word (Unbounded Bold, about 96px, lowercase), small word (Poppins Bold, about 44px), big word.
- Each big word on its own blue block, white; the last big word is secondary yellow. Small words are white over the footage.
- Two stacks back to back make the contrast. Keep word pairs together so the punchline reads in order ("better videos / same tools", never "better / videos same / tools").
- Centred, **top**.

### BIG NUMBER

- A money figure or count, Unbounded Bold, about 120px, white on a blue block. The money figure the story builds to is secondary yellow instead. Rounded and easy to read.
- Centred, **top**, for a second or two while it's said.

## TITLE (FIRST 3–5 SECONDS)

- Two lines, centred, **top**: a short lowercase lead-in in Poppins Bold (about 52px, white), saying what it is ("new Claude feature"), then the name in Unbounded Bold caps (about 76px, white on a blue block). No punctuation.
- When the video is about a brand's product, that brand's logo flies in from off screen several times around the title, drifts while it holds, and flies back out. Kept clear of the text and the speaker's head.
- Stays while the first sentences play, then cuts away.

## PICTURES

Whenever a company, app, place, product or scene is named, show it: a photo, or the brand's logo or app icon. Screen shares, clips and explainers go on a split instead (below).

- Laid right over the speaker's face, covering it. About 65% of the frame width, landscape, hard edges, no border, no shadow.
- Shifted left, centre or right from one picture to the next.
- Several in a row for a list, one per item, changing on the spoken word.
- The caption stays visible below.
- Real things only: real photos, real logos and app icons in their own colours. No illustrations.

## LAYOUTS

- **Full screen** (most of the video): speaker medium shot, chest up.
- **Split** (50/50), for anything he explains that isn't a single photo: a screen share or screenshot, a video clip, a motion graphic or explainer. Also when he points up or says "this" about something to show.
  - Top half: a cream or dark panel (see colours) with the thing on it, about 92% of the frame width, centred, rounded corners. The thing shown gets the space: big and high in the panel, never pushed down to make room for a label.
  - Bottom half: the speaker at his normal size, never zoomed in, moved up or down so the top of his head sits about 5% of the half's height (about 50px) under the seam. Look at the footage to find the top of the head (hair, cap or hat included) for each split.
  - Captions sit centred on the seam.
  - Cut in and out on a picture cut. Holds as long as he talks about what's shown, often ten seconds: the panel switches to the next thing on the spoken word, and the split ends with that topic. Things named during a split go on the panel, not over his face.
  - A clip from someone else's video freezes on its moment before it cuts to anything off-topic (a presenter, a transition).
  - When he names items on a panel's screenshot (plans, buttons), one thick red YouTube-style arrow with a white outline pops on at the first, landing just above the end of the word, glides fast (about 3 frames) to each next item as he names it, and goes when the panel changes; a light ding on each.
- **Behind**, when he talks about something with parts (a product reaching accounts, a hub and spokes): a motion graphic in the top half behind him, no split, him cut out in front (person masks). E.g. a big spinning brand logo with dotted lines running out to solid chips that light up on the spoken word.
- No insets or cutaways.

## PACING AND CUTS

- Tight jump cuts, no full stops: the words almost touch across a join (the next word starts about 30 ms after the last one dies away, over its fade).
- J-cuts at every join: the next line is heard about 160 ms before its picture shows; the old shot holds under it.
- Zoom (a punch-in, about 118%) only for two reasons: to hide a jump cut (takes alternate normal and punch-in), or to make a point on a key line. Never just to keep things moving. One framing per take otherwise.
- Splits never zoom: no punch-in while a split is on. They start and end on a picture cut.

## MOTION

- Captions and splits cut in and out.
- Everything else arrives with a small, quick entrance (a pop, a settle) and acts out the idea: lists build up item by item on the spoken word, what gets cut leaves, before turns into after. Never slow or floaty.
- Headlines land with a snap and then hold still while they're read. Stack words arrive one by one.
- Two bigger moments: the flying logos around the title, and a closing gag when the last line is about money (dollar bills raining down the whole frame).

## SOUND

- No music.
- Sound effects: yes, very subtle, felt more than heard, far under the voice. A soft pop when a picture or stack word appears, a soft click for the next picture in a run or a split panel changing, a soft swish when a split opens, a soft whoosh for flying logos. Made with ElevenLabs.
## ENDING

- The last sentence is the takeaway. No end card, no call to action on screen.
- When it's about money: real banknotes (public domain $100 scans, both sides) flutter down with depth (smaller, softer bills behind the speaker, big sharp ones in front, the speaker cut out between them), and a cash register ka-ching lands on "money" as the catch. The bills are already in the air when it cuts in, with none over his face, and never need to land. Hold the last shot about 0.7 s, ending before the speaker reaches for the camera.

## DON'T

- No yellow anywhere except the payoff.
- No boxes behind text except the blue headline blocks and the caption pill, no outlines.
- No moving text while it's read, no fades, no transitions between shots.
- No cartoons, clip art or emojis. Real logos and app icons are welcome.
